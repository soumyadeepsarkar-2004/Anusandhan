### Day 2 (Tuesday) — Feature Deep Dive: The 146-Word Rule
**Topic:** Why professors delete emails longer than 150 words.

```markdown
Why 146 words?

We analyzed faculty response rates across European and North American labs. The data is unequivocal:
• Emails > 300 words: < 3% response rate.
• Emails without paper citations: < 1.5% response rate.
• Emails with 120–150 words directly referencing a methodological gap: > 24% response rate.

Anusandhan's outreach synthesizer enforces a strict 146-word constraint:
- Paragraph 1 (30 words): Who you are + exact research interest alignment.
- Paragraph 2 (75 words): Deconstruction of their recent paper + what specific experiment or code you can contribute.
- Paragraph 3 (41 words): Clean ask with your pre-compiled Research Dossier link and zero flattery.

No generic templates. Just academic substance.

Try the synthesizer free: https://anusandhan.vercel.app
```

### Day 4 (Thursday) — Architecture: Offline-First Research Engine
**Topic:** Privacy and local sync (MongoDB + SQLite).

```markdown
Most academic tools force you to upload your confidential CVs and drafts to third-party cloud servers without transparency.

We built Anusandhan with an Offline-First, Privacy-First Architecture:

1. Local-First Persistence: All drafting, literature notes, and faculty bookmarks are saved locally on your device via an encrypted SQLite store.
2. Zero-Loss Sync: When your network drops, your work is never interrupted. Our offline sync engine queues transactions and reconciles idempotently with MongoDB Atlas the second you reconnect.
3. Edge Security: Cryptographic JWT authentication, Content Security Policy (CSP), and zero personal data monetization.

Research should be sovereign.
```

### Day 5 (Friday) — The 5 Pillars of RRS™ (Research Readiness Score)
**Topic:** How to know if you're truly ready to apply to top research labs.

```markdown
Before you reach out to a lab director at Max Planck, Oxford, or IISc, how do you evaluate your application readiness?

CGPA alone doesn't tell a lab director if you can run PyTorch experiments or deconstruct an algorithm.

We introduced the Research Readiness Score (RRS™) — a 5-pillar diagnostic:

1. Code Reproducibility: Clean GitHub commits, unit tests, reproducible environments.
2. Literature Literacy: Familiarity with foundational ArXiv/PubMed papers in your subfield.
3. Lab Tooling Fluency: Proficiency in Linux, Git, Docker, and domain-specific benchmarks.
4. Problem Statement Clarity: Ability to articulate a research gap without hand-waving.
5. Communication Precision: Concise, jargon-accurate technical writing.
```
